## What Is Stress?
"A state of worry or mental tension caused by a difficult situation." A natural human response that helps us address life's challenges and threats; everyone experiences some stress, but how we respond to it significantly affects well-being.

## How Stress Affects Us
Moderate stress can be beneficial and help us tackle daily tasks. Excessive stress creates problems for physical and mental health. Developing effective coping strategies reduces feeling overwhelmed and supports wellness.

## Common Signs and Symptoms
Difficulty relaxing, anxiety or irritability, concentration problems, physical symptoms (headaches, body pain, stomach upset, sleep disturbances), appetite changes (loss or increased eating), increased use of alcohol/tobacco/other substances. Chronic stress can worsen existing health conditions; persistent stress that interferes with work, school, or daily functioning may indicate a developing mental health condition like anxiety or depression.

## When to Seek Professional Help
Anyone struggling to manage stress should "seek help from a trusted health-care provider or from another trusted person in our community."

## Evidence-Based Stress Management Strategies
Structured daily routine (consistent schedules for meals, family time, exercise, chores, recreation); sleep prioritization (consistent bed/wake times, quiet/dark/cool room, limit screens before bed, avoid large meals/caffeine/alcohol before sleep, daytime exercise); social connection (stay in touch with family/friends, share concerns with trusted people); balanced nutrition with regular meals and adequate fluids; regular physical activity; limiting news consumption if it increases stress. WHO's "Doing What Matters in Times of Stress" guide offers practical self-help techniques requiring only minutes daily.
