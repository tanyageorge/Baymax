## Types of Burns (by severity/depth)
First-degree: damage only the outer layer of skin.
Second-degree: damage the outer layer and the layer underneath.
Third-degree: damage or destroy the deepest layer of skin and the tissues underneath.

## Causes
Heat, chemicals, electricity, sunlight, or radiation. Common sources: hot liquids, steam, building fires, flammable substances. Smoke inhalation also causes burn injuries.

## Home Care for Minor Burns
First aid for blisters and minor thermal injuries; aftercare instructions for minor burns at home; topical treatments — antibiotic creams can help prevent or treat infection.

## When to Seek Medical Care
Burns causing swelling, blistering, or scarring; injury involving shock; burns that damage the skin's protective barrier (raising infection risk); burns that are deep or cover a significant body surface area; situations requiring wound cleaning, skin replacement, or fluid/nutrition support. Professional evaluation is important for severe cases to guide wound management and prevent complications.
