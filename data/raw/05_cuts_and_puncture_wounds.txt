TITLE: Cuts and Puncture Wounds
SOURCE: MedlinePlus (https://www.medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000043.htm)
RETRIEVED: 2026-09-15

## Definition
A cut (laceration) is a break in the skin, which may be deep, smooth, or jagged. A puncture is a wound made by a pointed object (nail, knife, sharp tooth) — it can extend into deeper tissue despite appearing surface-level.

## Symptoms to Watch For
Bleeding, pain, loss of function or sensation (numbness, tingling) below the wound site.

## First Aid for Minor Cuts
1. Clean your hands with soap or antibacterial cleanser.
2. Wash the cut thoroughly with mild soap and water.
3. Apply direct pressure to stop bleeding.
4. Use antibacterial ointment and a non-stick bandage.

## First Aid for Minor Punctures
1. Clean your hands first.
2. Rinse under running water for 5 minutes, then wash with soap.
3. Look carefully for embedded objects without probing.
4. Apply antibacterial ointment and a non-stick bandage.
Important: if an object is stuck in the wound, or fragments may be missing, seek medical attention immediately.

## Do NOT
Assume minor wounds are clean without washing; breathe on open wounds; attempt cleaning major wounds yourself; remove deeply embedded objects; probe or pick at debris.

## Seek Emergency Care (Call 911) If:
Bleeding cannot be stopped after 10 minutes of pressure; the person cannot feel the area or it lacks normal function; severe overall injury exists.

## Contact a Doctor Right Away If:
Wound is large or deep (deeper than 1/4 inch, on the face, or reaches bone); from an animal or human bite; caused by a fishhook or rusty object; stepped on a nail; signs of infection appear (warmth, redness, fever, swelling, pus, red streaking); tetanus vaccination is overdue (needed every 10 years).
