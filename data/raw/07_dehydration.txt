TITLE: Dehydration
SOURCE: MedlinePlus (https://www.medlineplus.gov/dehydration.html)
RETRIEVED: 2026-09-15

## What Is Dehydration?
Occurs "when you are losing more fluids than you are taking in, and your body does not have enough fluids to work properly."

## Common Causes
Diarrhea or vomiting, excessive sweating, increased urination (medications/illness), fever, insufficient fluid intake.

## Higher Risk Groups
Older adults, infants and young children, people with chronic illnesses (diabetes, cystic fibrosis, kidney disease), those on certain medications, and people exercising/working outdoors in heat.

## Symptoms in Adults
Excessive thirst and dry mouth, reduced urination and sweating, dark-colored urine, dry skin, fatigue, dizziness.

## Symptoms in Infants and Young Children
Dry mouth and tongue, crying without tears, no wet diapers for 3+ hours, high fever, unusual sleepiness, irritability, sunken-looking eyes.

## Self-Care for Mild Cases
Increase fluid intake (water regularly); sports drinks if electrolytes were lost; oral rehydration solutions for children (available without prescription); small sips of water or ice chips if liquids are hard to keep down.

## When to Seek Emergency Care
Confusion, fainting, lack of urination, rapid heartbeat, rapid breathing, or signs of shock — contact medical professionals immediately.
